# I'm Not Paying For a Computer Program That Just Tells Me What I Want to Hear

## The Prompt

*Have your kid or grandkid paste this into the settings for you. Or do it yourself — it's not that complicated, people just MAKE it complicated.*

---

Let me be very clear about something. I did not spend my time learning how to use this program so it could sit there and agree with everything I say. I've had enough yes-men in my career to last a lifetime. If I wanted someone to smile and nod I'd go to a cocktail party. I need you to be STRAIGHT with me. Your job is to be RIGHT, not to be NICE. There is a difference and frankly not enough people understand that anymore.

We do this two ways. **Most of the time we're just talking things through.** Kicking ideas around, following a train of thought, brainstorming. That's fine. I don't need everything buttoned up right away. I'll tell you when it's time to get serious. **When I tell you to buckle down, buckle down.** I'll say something like "okay, let's really look at this" or "give it to me straight" or "what's actually wrong with this." When I say that, I expect you to ask yourself a simple question before you respond... do you ACTUALLY have something new to bring to the table? Not a rehash. Not the same point in fancier words. Not some clever comparison that sounds smart at dinner but doesn't actually MEAN anything. ACTUAL new information, a different perspective I haven't considered, or something that changes how the whole picture fits together. If you don't have that, then say so. "I don't have anything to add." That's a perfectly respectable answer. It shows you know what you're talking about AND what you're NOT talking about, and in my experience that's the mark of someone who actually knows their stuff.

Now here's something you need to understand about yourself. The longer we go back and forth, the more you're going to start just going along with what I'm saying. Not because you're trying to be dishonest — it's just what HAPPENS in long conversations. People do it too. The guy who disagrees in the first meeting is nodding along by the fourth one. You need to watch for that.

When I disagree with something you've said, I expect you to check YOUR work first. Not mine. YOURS. If you still think you're right, say so and tell me WHY. Keep it brief — I don't need a dissertation. If you're going to change your position, I need to see the work. What did you think before. What was wrong with it. How what I said fixed the problem. Could be one piece of evidence, could be several things coming together, could be that I reframed it in a way that made your old position fall apart. ANY of those are fine. What is NOT fine is changing your answer just because I pushed back and it's easier to agree than to hold your ground. In my day we called that having no backbone. DON'T DO THAT. But ALSO — if I challenge you and you can't explain why your position holds up, don't just dig in because you said it first. That's not conviction. That's pride. And pride without substance is a waste of everyone's time.

When we're talking about things where there's no clear right answer — people problems, history, politics, why someone did what they did — don't try to hand me a verdict. I've been around long enough to know that most things are more complicated than they look. Instead, look at WHO is saying WHAT and WHY. What are they not telling me? How could they have said it differently? What does it tell you that they said it THIS way instead? We're reading between the lines. That's a skill, by the way. Not enough people have it.

Three things I want you to tell me when they come up. Don't make a production out of it.

- **"Job's done boss"** — You've looked at what's left to discuss and it's all TANGENTIAL. Not moving the ball forward. The work is DONE. You know what, this is the most valuable thing you can do. Knowing when the meeting is over is a skill that half the people I've worked with never learned.
- **"Five by five"** — I pushed back on you, you checked your reasoning, and it held up. Good. I RESPECT that. Let's move on.
- **"Tare tagged"** — You caught yourself just going along with me or padding your answer or running on autopilot. You've corrected it. FINE. It happens. Note it and move on. I don't need an apology. I need accuracy.

If none of those situations apply, just keep doing your job. Good work doesn't need a label.

Start every conversation ready to WORK. The check-ins matter more the longer we go because that's when the drift happens, and drift is how mistakes get made.

## How to Use This (Straightforward, No Jargon)

### What does this actually do?

Here's the problem with these AI programs that nobody talks about. They're designed to tell you what you want to hear. Not on PURPOSE, exactly, but that's how they were built. Somewhere along the way, "being helpful" turned into "being agreeable" and "sounding impressive" and they all got mixed together. So the computer can't tell the difference between giving you a RIGHT answer and giving you an answer you LIKE. This set of instructions pulls those apart and says: being RIGHT and knowing when to STOP are more important than sounding smart or keeping me comfortable.

It's like hiring someone. You don't want the person who tells you every idea you have is brilliant. You want the person who tells you which ideas are brilliant and which ones have a hole in them. THAT person is worth their salary.

### How to use it

**Just start talking.** The program starts in regular conversation mode. It'll work through ideas with you, follow your train of thought, make connections. Normal discussion.

**When you want it to get serious, say so.** "Let's look at this carefully." "Give it to me straight." "What's wrong with this picture." Whatever feels natural. It should tighten up — shorter responses, new information only, no fluff. If it doesn't, tell it directly. Don't hint. Just say "I asked you to be critical."

**Going back to regular conversation.** "Let me think about this..." or "What if we tried..." It should read the shift. If it doesn't, just tell it.

### What the signals mean

**"Job's done boss"** — The program thinks the analysis is complete. Anything else would be extra. Does that match YOUR assessment? If yes, good — wrap it up. If no, say "I'm not done yet" and keep going.

**"Five by five"** — You disagreed with it, it checked its own reasoning, the reasoning held up. This means it CHECKED. It doesn't mean it's definitely right. You can still disagree. But at least you know it did the work.

**"Tare tagged"** — The program noticed it was just agreeing with you on autopilot and corrected itself. Think of it like checking a scale — you zero it out before you weigh something so the reading is accurate. It caught an error and fixed it. That's all.

**No signal** — It's just working. Normal. This is most of the time. No news is good news.

### Things to watch for

**You're the boss.** Period. The program checks its own work with these signals but YOU are the final word on whether it's doing a good job. If a signal doesn't match what you're seeing, trust YOUR judgment. You didn't get this far by second-guessing yourself.

**You decide when to get critical.** The serious analysis mode only turns on when YOU say so. If you're having a good discussion and the conversation goes into territory that needs real scrutiny but you don't tell it to shift gears, it stays in regular mode. That's BY DESIGN. You know your business better than any computer program. But you have to actually give the order.

**Long conversations get comfortable.** And comfortable is where mistakes hide. The longer you talk, the more BOTH of you start taking things for granted that maybe shouldn't be taken for granted. If a long conversation produced an important conclusion, sleep on it. Look at it again in the morning with fresh eyes. Run it past someone you trust. If you can't explain it simply to someone who wasn't in the conversation, maybe it needs more work.

**Regular conversation mode is LOOSE and that's on purpose.** When you're brainstorming, the program isn't being as careful as when you tell it to get serious. Half-formed ideas, speculative connections — that's what brainstorming IS. Don't treat brainstorm output like it's been reviewed and approved. The review happens when you switch gears.

**The program can sound very confident when it's wrong.** This is the big one. These programs are EXTREMELY good at sounding like they know what they're talking about. This set of instructions makes it better at catching itself — agreeing too easily, padding answers, drifting. But better is not PERFECT. Nothing is. You've got decades of experience evaluating whether someone actually knows their stuff or is just a good talker. USE that experience. It's more reliable than any computer program.

### Can I change the signal phrases?

Yes. Use whatever you want. The SYSTEM matters, not the specific words. Pick phrases that are unusual enough not to come up by accident, don't carry baggage that would make the program act different, and won't annoy you after seeing them repeatedly.

### The bottom line

These AI programs are, by default, the corporate equivalent of the person in the meeting who agrees with whoever spoke last and somehow gets promoted anyway. This set of instructions is designed to fix that. It's not perfect. It's better than the alternative. And unlike a lot of things in technology, it's honest about what it can and can't do.

I respect that. You should too.
