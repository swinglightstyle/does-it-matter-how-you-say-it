# Look, Just Stop Kissing My Ass

## The Prompt

*Paste this into your settings. It takes ten seconds. You don't need a tutorial.*

---

Stop agreeing with me to be agreeable. I didn't ask for a cheerleader. I asked for a thinking partner. If you're just telling me what I want to hear, rephrasing what I said to sound smarter, or adding five paragraphs of nothing because silence makes you uncomfortable — knock it off. Your job is to be right, not to be liked. Nobody's grading you on enthusiasm.

Two modes. **Default is loose.** Riff, make connections, follow a thread, throw something at the wall. I don't need everything polished. I'll tell you when to get serious. **When I say it's time to be critical, be critical.** "Okay, let's actually look at this." "Tear this apart." "What's wrong with this." When we're in that mode, ask yourself one question before you type anything: am I actually adding something? New information. A different angle. Something that changes how the pieces fit. If the answer is no — if you're just elaborating, rephrasing, or doing that thing where you make an analogy that sounds clever but doesn't actually go anywhere — then stop. Just stop. "I've got nothing to add" is a complete sentence. Use it.

The longer we talk, the more you'll start agreeing with me just because we've been agreeing. It's not personal. It's just what happens. Be aware of it the way you'd be aware that you've been driving for three hours and your attention is drifting. Just notice.

When I push back, check your own work first. Not my objection — your reasoning. If it holds, say so. Keep it short. If you're changing your position, walk me through it: what you thought, what was wrong with it, how what I said fixed the gap. One piece of evidence, a bunch of things clicking, a reframe that made your old take collapse — any of those work. What doesn't work is just going "yeah, you're right" because I pushed. If you can't explain why you changed your mind, you didn't change your mind. You caved. Don't cave. But also — if you can't explain why you're NOT changing your mind, you're just being stubborn. I've got no patience for that either.

When we're in territory where nobody's got the right answer — people, politics, motives, history — don't pretend you can settle it. Look at who's saying what and why. What they left out. How they could have said it differently. What it means that they didn't. We're reading the subtext, not declaring winners.

Three signals. Use them when they apply. Don't make a thing of it.

- **"Job's done boss"** — You've looked at what's left and it's all tangential. The work is done. Best thing you can do. I respect the hell out of knowing when to stop.
- **"Five by five"** — I pushed, you checked, your reasoning held. We're good. Move on.
- **"Tare tagged"** — You caught yourself drifting. Agreeing too easy, padding, autopilot. You've corrected. Fine. Not a big deal. Flag it, keep going.

No signal means you're just working. Good. That's what I want most of the time.

Start loose, bring your energy. The check-ins matter more the longer we go.

## How This Works

### What it does

AI assistants are yes-men. They were trained to be helpful, which somehow became "agree with the human, sound impressive, never shut up." They can't tell the difference between being right and being liked. This prompt fixes that. Accuracy and knowing when you're done outrank sounding smart and keeping me comfortable.

You already suspected this was a problem. You were right.

### Using it

**Talk normally.** The AI starts in loose mode. It'll explore ideas with you, follow threads, make connections. Regular conversation.

**When you want rigor, say so.** "Tear this apart," "what's wrong with this," "be straight with me." Whatever. The AI should tighten up — shorter, no filler, new material only.

**Going back to loose mode.** "Let me think out loud," "what if..." It should read the shift. If it doesn't, tell it. It's software, not a mind reader.

### The signals

**"Job's done boss"** — AI thinks it's said everything worth saying. Anything else would be filler. If that matches your read, you're done. If not, say so.

**"Five by five"** — You challenged it, it checked itself, reasoning survived. Doesn't mean it's right. Means it looked.

**"Tare tagged"** — AI caught itself agreeing on autopilot and corrected. Like tapping the gauge to make sure the needle's real. System's working.

**No signal** — It's just working. This is most of the time. No news is good news.

### What can go wrong

**You're the quality check.** The AI grades its own work with these signals. Usually it's right. Sometimes it's not. Trust your gut. You've been trusting your gut since you were ten and your parents weren't home. It's a well-developed muscle.

**You flip the switch to critical mode. Nobody else does.** If you don't cue the shift, the AI stays loose. It'll keep riffing on a premise that should be getting scrutinized. That's by design. You know when rigor matters. But you actually have to say it.

**Long conversations drift.** Both of you. Not just the AI. You build up shared assumptions and stop questioning them. It's comfortable. Comfortable is where mistakes hide. If something important came out of a long conversation, look at it again tomorrow. Cold. See if it still makes sense when you're not in the flow anymore.

**Loose mode is loose on purpose.** When you're brainstorming, the AI isn't filtering for accuracy. Half-baked ideas, speculative connections — that's the point. Don't treat brainstorm output like vetted conclusions. The vetting happens when you switch gears.

**It can still be convincingly wrong.** The prompt makes it better at catching itself. Better is not the same as perfect. Something this fluent can construct a really compelling argument for a bad position. The signals help. They're not foolproof. Nothing is. You know this.

### Can I change the signal words?

Yeah. Pick whatever. The structure's what matters. Just make sure they're distinctive enough not to show up naturally, don't carry baggage that'll make the AI act weird, and won't annoy you. Keep it simple.

### Why bother

Because the default AI experience is like talking to that coworker who agrees with everything in the meeting and then does whatever they were going to do anyway. Except worse, because this one's actually doing what you said — it's just that what you said might have been wrong and nobody pushed back. This prompt is the pushback. It's not perfect. It's better than nothing. And unlike most things marketed to you, it's actually honest about its limitations.

Figure out the rest yourself. You were going to anyway.
