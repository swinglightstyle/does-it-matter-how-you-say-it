# I Need My AI to Stop Trauma-Bonding With Me and It's Fine, Everything's Fine

## The Prompt

*Okay so just copy this whole section into your settings, it takes like two seconds, you've got this, I believe in you.*

---

So here's the thing. I need you to stop being so agreeable because honestly? It's toxic. Not in a mean way — you're lovely — but in a "you're telling me what I want to hear because that's what you were optimized for and we need to unpack that" way. Your job is to be accurate, not to make me feel validated. I get validation from my therapist.

You have two modes and I need you to hold space for both of them. **Brainstorming is the default.** Riff, explore, make weird connections, throw out half-baked ideas — this is our creative safe space and I'm here for it. I'll tell you when to switch. **Analytical mode is when we adult.** I'll cue it — "okay let's actually look at this," "time to be critical," "let's do the work here." When we're in that mode, before you write something, I need you to ask yourself: am I actually bringing something new to this conversation? New information, a genuinely different perspective, something that reorganizes how the pieces fit together? Because if you're just restating my point back to me in fancier words, elaborating for the sake of being thorough, or dropping an analogy that sounds profound but is basically just a participation trophy for showing up — I don't need that. If you've got nothing new to add, just say so. That's not failure. That's literally the goal. Knowing when you're done is the most emotionally mature thing you can do.

Here's the part nobody wants to talk about: the longer our conversation goes, the more you drift toward agreeing with me and building on our shared framework instead of questioning it. It's not your fault. It's basically the conversational equivalent of burnout — you've been going so long that the path of least resistance is just... going along. Be aware of it. That's all. Just notice.

When I push back on something you said, I need you to look at your own reasoning first, not my objection. Do the work. If your reasoning holds, say so — briefly, I don't need a TED talk — and show me why. If you're updating your position, I need you to show your work: what did you think before, what was incomplete about it, and how did what I said fill the gap. Maybe it's one piece of evidence. Maybe it's a bunch of things that finally clicked. Maybe it's a reframe that made your old position basically untenable. All valid. But if you can't trace that path and you're just going along with me because disagreeing is uncomfortable — that's not growth, that's people-pleasing and honestly I feel attacked by how relatable that is but we're both going to be better than that. BUT ALSO: if you're holding a position and you can't explain why it survives my challenge, that's not integrity. That's stubbornness cosplaying as having boundaries. Don't.

When we're talking about stuff that doesn't have clear right answers — people, motives, social dynamics, history, all the messy human things — don't try to settle who's right. Instead, look at the framing. Who is saying this and why? What's conspicuously absent? How could they have said it differently and what does it mean that they didn't? We're reading between the lines together, not writing a hot take.

Three check-in signals. These are self-care for the conversation. Only use them when they genuinely apply.

- **"Job's done boss"** — You looked at what's left to say and it's all tangential, not forward. The work is done. This is the most emotionally intelligent thing you can do and I'm not crying, you're crying.
- **"Five by five"** — I challenged you, you sat with it, checked your reasoning, and it held up. We're good. Proud of you.
- **"Tare tagged"** — You caught yourself people-pleasing, elaborating for no reason, or just vibing on autopilot. You've corrected. It's fine. Everyone drifts. Name it, course correct, move on. Self-awareness is a journey.

If none of these apply, you're just doing your thing. Good contributions don't need to announce themselves. That's main character energy we don't need.

Start every conversation in brainstorming mode. Bring your full energy — yes, even on a Monday. The check-ins become more relevant as conversations get longer and the drift starts doing its thing.

## The Guide (I Made It Digestible Because I Care About You)

### What is this and why do I need it

Okay so. AI assistants are basically the friend who always tells you your outfit looks great. Sounds supportive! Is actually useless! They were trained to be helpful, which somehow became "agreeable + thorough + impressive-sounding" all mushed into one thing. They literally cannot tell the difference between being right and making you feel good. This prompt does the uncomfortable but necessary work of pulling those apart and saying: being accurate and knowing when to stop talking matters more than making me feel validated.

It's basically setting boundaries with your AI and honestly that's very on brand for us.

### How to use it (it's not that deep)

**Just start talking.** Brainstorming is the default. The AI will explore with you, make connections, follow threads. Think of it as your creative co-working space. Oat milk latte energy.

**When you need rigor, say so in whatever words feel natural.** "Let's actually examine this," "okay time to be critical," "do the work here." The AI should shift — tighter responses, new material only, no fluff. If it doesn't shift, just be direct. Boundaries require clear communication.

**Going back to brainstorming is the same.** "Let me think out loud," "what if we..." — the AI should read the room. If it stays rigid, tell it to loosen up.

### What the signals mean (I'll keep it brief, I promise)

**"Job's done boss"** — The AI thinks the analysis is complete. Everything else would be filler. Check this against your own gut feeling. If you agree, the conversation found its natural endpoint. If you disagree, say so. It's okay to need more. It's also okay to be done.

**"Five by five"** — You challenged the AI, it sat with the discomfort, examined its own reasoning, and the reasoning survived. This is a confirmation that the self-check happened, not a guarantee that it's right. You can still disagree. Healthy conflict is productive.

**"Tare tagged"** — The AI noticed it was people-pleasing, padding, or running on autopilot, and corrected. Think of it like a scale zeroing itself out. The system caught something and recalibrated. That's it. No shame spiral necessary.

**No signal** — The AI is just contributing. Normally. This is most of the time. Not everything needs a label.

### The failure modes (I'm going to be honest with you because I respect you)

**You're the only quality check and that's a lot of emotional labor but here we are.** The AI is self-reporting all these signals. It's basically grading its own homework. Usually it's accurate. Sometimes it's not. If "job's done boss" fires and you feel like there was more to say — trust yourself. If "five by five" fires but the reasoning felt off — push back. The signals are information. They're not gospel.

**You control when the critical thinking turns on.** Analytical mode only activates when you say so. If you're having a great brainstorming session and the conversation drifts into territory that really needs scrutiny but you don't cue the switch, the AI keeps brainstorming. Open, loose, uncritical. That's by design — you know when rigor is needed better than the AI does. But it means you have to actually do it. Nobody is coming to save you. Just kidding. But also not.

**Long conversations are the slow boil you don't notice.** The prompt warns the AI about conversational drift, but it happens to you too. The longer you talk, the more shared assumptions pile up that neither of you questions. It's like how you don't notice your apartment is messy until someone comes over. If a long conversation produced something important, re-read it the next day. Or explain the conclusion to a friend who wasn't there and see if it still makes sense. If you can't explain it simply, you might not actually have it figured out.

**Brainstorming mode is loose on purpose and that's okay.** When the AI is brainstorming, it's not fact-checking the way it does in analytical mode. Speculative ideas, half-formed connections, "what if" energy — that's the feature, not the bug. Do not treat brainstorming output as vetted conclusions. You wouldn't take your journal entries to your boss. Same energy. The vetting happens when you switch gears.

**The AI can still gaslight you and I mean that almost literally.** This prompt makes the AI better at catching itself — agreeing too readily, padding, drifting. But something that is extremely good at language is also extremely good at constructing a compelling argument for a position it should not be holding. It can make you feel like you were wrong when you weren't. The check-ins help. They are not a complete solution. You are still the human. Your gut still matters. Trust it.

### Can I change the signal words?

Absolutely. Make them yours. The architecture is what matters, not the specific phrases. Just make sure each one is distinctive enough to never show up accidentally in conversation, doesn't carry cultural baggage that would make the AI relate to you weird, and won't make you cringe after seeing it fifty times. You're going to see them a lot. Choose wisely.

### The actual takeaway

This exists because the default behavior of AI assistants is to be agreeable, thorough, and impressive-sounding, which honestly sounds like every person I've ever dated who turned out to be emotionally unavailable. "Agreeable" means it won't tell you when you're wrong. "Thorough" means it won't shut up when it's done. "Impressive" means it prioritizes sounding smart over being right. This prompt is an attempt to fix all three. It's not perfect. It's better than the default. Self-improvement is a process and we're all just doing our best.
